## 2. Three primaries are the origin of everything (cohesion comes from shared origin)
Winsor & Newton: **Cobalt Blue** + **Permanent Alizarin Crimson** + **Yellow Ochre** (Series 1).
(Earlier earth triad also valid: French Ultramarine 263 / Venetian Red 678 / Yellow Ochre 744.)
Everything is mixed from these → every colour belongs to one family → any colour pairs with any other.

## 3. Light vs shadow
- **LIGHT** = the subject / the "yellow strokes" / highlights = ONE or TWO primaries (clean).
- **SHADOW** = a **MUTED** tone of the same colour — *not necessarily dark*. The **arsenic principle**: a *minuscule* drop of the THIRD primary mutes the entire mix into shadow, like one drop of arsenic poisoning ten litres of water.
- Build living colour by walking LIGHT → its own 3-primary mixed SHADOW. **Never darken with black by default** (black only when a genuinely dark tone is required). **White** lightens freely (it changes value, never moves a 3-pigment mix back to "light").
- Mute GREENS with a whisper of Alizarin; mute BLUES with a whisper of **Yellow Ochre — NEVER Alizarin** (Cobalt + Alizarin + white = **periwinkle**, which the founder DESPISES).

## 4. Work in GROUPS
Build page colours as a group sharing one origin + one muting (e.g. green group: Ochre-led + Cobalt; blue group: Cobalt-led + Ochre). Any member pairs with any other and with another same-origin group. Cohesion = shared origin, not matching hue.

### 4b. Two locked colours in one environment ⇒ derive their match system (founder, 2026-06-27)
When two already-locked colours must share ONE setting / graph / environment — e.g. the What-If
**emerald `#2ECC71`** and the **lemon yellow `#EBD60A`** together — they imply a full three-primary
match system. Derive it; do not pick the partners freely:
1. **Anchor on the lemon yellow** (`#EBD60A`).
2. **Find the blue** that, mixed with that yellow, produces **this exact green** (`#2ECC71`) — that is
   "the blue that must go with" the pair. You now hold the yellow + the blue.
3. **Find the red** that tones in the **right gradient** with those two (yellow + blue).
4. Yellow + blue + red = **a new match system** for that environment; every other colour in it is mixed
   from this origin (the shared-origin rule, §4).
Claude derives the blue/red **by the method** (never a colour question) and **pins to the founder's
physical mix as truth** — screen hexes are approximations.
